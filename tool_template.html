Busy ECM weeks don’t show up in UK placing discounts.

An extra £0.43bn raised in other UK equity offerings in the week before launch, a typical busy week, moved placing discounts by about 1bp. A cost above 1.3pp is ruled out at 95% confidence. In this sample, deeper discounts go with an AIM listing and with raises that are large relative to the company.

Comparables are the eight placings on the same market closest in deal size and share of capital. If you pick a use of proceeds, placings with the same use rank as closer matches. Placings that did not publish their share of capital count as less similar.

Eight deals give a wide range, so read the 25th to 75th percentile rows as much as the median. This is a reference point from past deals, not a forecast or a pricing view, and it does not adjust for market conditions at the time.

The raw gap is 0.7pp and not significant (t = −0.45). It also points the wrong way for a congestion story: the busiest weeks priced slightly tighter.

“Busy” mostly means 2020. 62% of busy-third deals launched that year, and from 2023 almost none qualify. A raw split therefore compares years, not weeks, which is why Exhibit 1 compares each deal only with deals in the same month.

Changing the window, counting deals instead of pounds, dropping 2020, dropping flagged deals or using only discounts stated in announcements leaves every estimate inside the primary interval.

The subgroup tests ask whether a cost appears where it would be most likely: on AIM, in larger deals, in weak markets and in less liquid stocks. None does, but those intervals are wide. These are results where no effect was detected, not proof that there is none.

Raw, AIM placings averaged a 10.6% discount against 5.6% on the Main Market. 391bp of that gap survives the controls, about £1.2m on a £30m raise.

Raises of 20% of share capital priced about 215bp deeper than raises of 8%. Deal size in pounds, once relative size is controlled for, is not significant.

Open offers priced 6.5pp deeper (p = 0.004). That result is exploratory and probably reflects the type of issuer that uses them.

Method

Discount: issue price against the last close before launch, using that day’s close for announcements after 16:35 and the previous day’s otherwise. A negative figure is a premium.

Supply: pounds raised in other UK equity offerings of £10m or more (placings, rights issues, open offers, IPOs) in the five trading days before launch, relative to its 250-day average. OLS with year-month, segment and industry fixed effects; errors clustered by week. Single-investor and controller-led deals are excluded.

Data and checks

Every deal was built from launch announcements on the FCA National Storage Mechanism and London Stock Exchange new issue statistics, including companies that have since delisted.

Every discount was recomputed from the announcement and the last close before launch. On the 124 deals where the company stated its own discount, the two agree with a correlation of 0.99.

Limits

Placings are pre-sounded, so a congestion cost may show in whether or how much is raised rather than in price; that is untested. All supply is weighted equally, though a £2bn rights issue and a £15m AIM raise draw on different books. Sell-downs are missing (no free source), which likely biases the estimate toward zero. The Main Market sample is thin after 2021.

Open question: shares returned 1.9pp less than the market over the 20 trading days after launch, per typical busy week (p = 0.14, not significant; companies still listed only).

Terms used

Placing: a listed company selling new shares to investors, usually within a day. Discount: how far below the last market price the shares were sold. bp: a basis point, 0.01 percentage points; 100bp = 1pp. AIM: London’s market for smaller, growth companies; the Main Market is for larger ones. Busy week: an extra £0.43bn of other UK share sales in the week before launch, a typical swing. 95% interval: the range the true effect very likely falls in. Significant: unlikely to be chance at the 5% level.
